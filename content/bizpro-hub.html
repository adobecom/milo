Nutrition of Teenage Girls and Women in Humanitarian Crises

Global humanitarian crises have reached unprecedented scale and complexity, with the world now facing the most severe food and nutrition crisis in modern history. In just three years since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance has surged from 168 million to an alarming 339 million.

Humanitarian crises are growing more severe, disruptive, and prolonged than ever before. Many countries are confronting overlapping shocks—conflict and instability, extreme weather events like extended droughts or severe flooding, and disease outbreaks such as cholera.

All of this places a disproportionate burden on adolescent girls and women, worsening their ability to find, access, and afford nutritious food and essential nutrition services.

By Dr. Heather Esme-Collins MBBS, MRCPCH, MMed OVER, UNDER: NOURISHMENT AT A GLANCE
